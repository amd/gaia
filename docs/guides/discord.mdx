<Info>

</Info>

Send GAIA a private message on Discord from your phone, and it answers from your PC. You can
ask about your files, send it a document to read, have it look something up, or ask it to
keep an eye on something and tell you when it changes.

GAIA does the work on your PC, so the PC has to be on. Discord only carries the messages.

## Before you start

You need:

- **A Discord account** and the Discord app on your phone (or any device you'll chat from).
- **GAIA installed on your PC**, with the GAIA app or the GAIA terminal app.
- **About five minutes.** You'll create your own private Discord bot. It's free, and you don't
  need to know anything about bots: GAIA opens each page for you and tells you what to click.

<Note>
  A *bot* is just a Discord account that a program controls. Yours is controlled only by GAIA on
  your PC. A *DM* (direct message) is a private chat between two accounts, here you and your bot.
</Note>

## Set it up

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="GAIA app">
    1. Open **Settings → Messaging → Discord**.
    2. **Create your bot.** Click **Open Discord Developer Portal** and sign in with your
       Discord account if asked. Click **New Application**, name it *GAIA*, and click
       **Create**. Then click **Bot** in the left menu, click **Reset Token**, and click
       **Copy**. The copied text is your bot's *token*, which works like its password.
    3. **Connect it.** Back in GAIA, paste the token and click **Connect**. GAIA keeps it in your
       PC's secure password store. This can take up to a minute.

       <Frame caption="Steps 2 and 3: create the bot, then paste its token.">
         <img src="/images/discord/messaging-none.png" alt="Settings, Messaging, Discord card with a button to open the Discord Developer Portal and a box to paste the bot token" />
       </Frame>
    4. **Add the bot to a private server.** Discord only lets you message a bot that shares a
       server with you, so make a server just for this: in Discord, click **+** in the server
       list, then **Create My Own**, then **For me and my friends**. Back in GAIA, click
       **Open invite page** and pick that server. The bot can't do anything in the server;
       it's only there so you can message it.
    5. **Claim the bot.** GAIA shows a short code. Click **Open the bot in Discord**, click
       **Message**, and send the code. Whoever sends the code becomes the only person GAIA
       answers, so send it yourself.

       <Frame caption="Steps 4 and 5: invite the bot to your server, then send it the code.">
         <img src="/images/discord/messaging-pairing.png" alt="Discord card showing the invite button and a one-time code with a Copy code button" />
       </Frame>

    When the screen says **Answering *you***, you're done. Say hi to your bot.

    <Frame caption="Set up: GAIA answers your Discord messages from this PC.">
      <img src="/images/discord/messaging-connected.png" alt="Discord card saying Answering, with Open in Discord, Stop and Pair a different account buttons" />
    </Frame>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Terminal app">
    1. Type `/discord setup`. Your browser opens the Discord Developer Portal.
    2. Click **New Application**, name it *GAIA*, and click **Create**. Then click **Bot**,
       **Reset Token** and **Copy**.
    3. Paste the token when the terminal app asks for it. It stays hidden as you paste.
    4. Your browser opens the invite page. Add the bot to a private server: see step 4 of the
       **GAIA app** tab.
    5. Open the bot from the link shown, click **Message**, and send the code shown.

    When the terminal app says **Paired with *you***, you're done.
  </Tab>

<Tip>
  To stop strangers from adding your bot to their own servers, open the bot's page in the
  Developer Portal and turn off **Public Bot**. GAIA wouldn't answer them anyway; this just
  keeps the bot to yourself.
</Tip>

## Chatting with GAIA

Message your bot the way you'd talk to GAIA on your PC:

- "What's in my Downloads folder?"
- "Summarize this PDF" (with the file attached)
- "Write today's meeting notes to notes.md"
- "Are there new comments on my pull request?"

A few things that help:

- **Files go both ways.** Attach a file and GAIA can read it. When GAIA makes a file, takes a
  screenshot or generates an image, it sends it back in the chat. You can also ask for any
  file on the PC: "send me my resume from Documents". Files over 10 MB, key or password
  files, and anything in a hidden or app-settings folder (where apps keep sign-ins) stay on
  the PC.
- **Reply to a message** with Discord's Reply and GAIA knows which one you mean: "do this
  one".
- **Start fresh** by sending *new chat*. GAIA forgets the conversation so far, but keeps what
  you asked it to remember and all your reminders and alerts.
- **Send corrections as a new message.** A quick follow-up such as "actually, only the
  count" is answered together with the message before it. Editing a message you already
  sent doesn't change anything, and GAIA tells you so.
- **To stop GAIA,** press **Stop** under "Thinking…", or send *stop*.
- **Long answers** arrive as several messages.
- **Send *help*** for a short list of what you can say.
- **After GAIA restarts** (the PC rebooted, or you pressed Stop and Start) it begins a new
  conversation, and says so under its first answer.

## GAIA asks before it changes anything

Reading your files and looking things up don't need your OK. Before GAIA changes something,
such as running a program, saving or deleting a file, or posting on GitHub, it sends you the
exact action with buttons:

- **Allow** does it once.
- **Deny** skips it.
- **Always allow** appears for some actions, so GAIA stops asking about that kind of action
  from then on.

You can also just reply *yes* or *no*. If you don't answer within ten minutes, GAIA treats it
as *no*. Send *retry* to run that request again.

<Warning>
  Whoever controls the paired Discord account can see anything GAIA can read on your PC,
  which by default is everything in your user folder. Keep your Discord account secure, and
  turn on two-factor authentication in Discord's settings.
</Warning>

## Reminders, summaries and alerts

GAIA can message you on its own. Just ask:

- "Remind me at 5pm to call the dentist."
- "Every weekday at 8am, summarize what changed in my project."
- "Tell me right away when someone opens an issue on github.com/my-org/my-app."
- "Check in every 30 minutes, but only message me if something needs my attention."

GAIA confirms each one before it sets it up. To see or change them later, ask "what are you
watching?", "pause the summaries", or "stop the 5pm reminder". On the PC, **Settings →
Automations** in the GAIA app lists your reminders, summaries, alerts and triggers, and
pauses, resumes or removes one. GitHub watches are changed by asking. See
[Automations](/guides/automations) for more examples.

## Privacy

- Your messages, and GAIA's replies, pass through Discord's servers like any other Discord
  chat. Don't send passwords or other secrets. If GAIA ever needs one, it asks you to type it on
  the PC instead.
- The work itself happens on your PC: your files stay there unless GAIA sends you one.
- GAIA ignores every server channel, even in your private server. Only your DMs reach it.

## Stopping, pausing and disconnecting

All of these are in **Settings → Messaging → Discord** (or type `/discord` in the terminal
app):

- **Stop** pauses GAIA on Discord, and it stays off until you press **Start**, even after
  you reopen GAIA.
- **Restart** stops and starts it in one step.
- **Ask me again for all of these** appears once you've chosen **Always allow** for
  something. It lists what you allowed and clears the list, so GAIA asks again.
- **Pair a different account** shows a new code, so another Discord account can take over.
  This also clears every **Always allow** you chose.
- **Disconnect** forgets the bot and the paired account. Your bot still exists in the
  Developer Portal, and you can delete it there.

## If something goes wrong

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="GAIA doesn't answer my messages">
    Check that the PC is on and awake. After the PC restarts, open the GAIA app or the
    terminal app once, and GAIA starts answering again. You can close it afterwards. A
    message sent while the PC was off or asleep isn't run later; GAIA says so, and you can
    send it again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The bot doesn't reply to the code">
    Make sure you sent it as a direct message to the bot, not in the server. Codes expire
    after an hour or a few wrong tries: click **Show a pairing code** and send the new one.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Discord no longer accepts this token">
    The token was reset, or the bot was deleted. In the Developer Portal, open your
    application, click **Bot → Reset Token → Copy**, and paste the new token in Settings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Another PC is answering this bot">
    The same bot is connected on two PCs, and only one can answer. Click **Answer from this
    PC** on the one you want, or create a second bot for the other PC.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="GAIA's Discord connector already uses this bot">
    That bot is already used for a different GAIA feature. Create a second application in
    the Developer Portal and use its token here.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="GAIA couldn't read the saved bot token">
    Your PC's password store is locked. On Linux, log in to your desktop session; then click
    **Try again**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Good to know

- GAIA answers one Discord account per PC. It's one assistant with one conversation, so a
  second person would see the first person's chat.
- It works in direct messages only: no server channels, voice or slash commands.
