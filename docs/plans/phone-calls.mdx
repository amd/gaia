---
title: Phone calls and texts
description: GAIA texts or calls your phone when something urgent needs you, talks with you in voice mode over the call, and later takes your calls, all with the models still on your PC
---

**Status: Design for a later release. Not part of the current release, and implementation has not started.**
Baseline: `aaa056b7` on `main`, November 2, 2026. Builds on [voice mode](/plans/voice), which must ship first.

A notification is easy to miss. A text is harder to miss, and a phone call is hardest. This plan
lets GAIA text you or ring your phone when something you marked as urgent happens: a scheduled
job failed, an email from someone on your list arrived, or a task has waited on your approval
too long. A text says what happened in one line. On a call, GAIA tells you in a sentence or two,
you can ask follow-up questions in voice mode, and the call is saved as a chat you can read
later.

**The models stay on your PC.** Speech recognition, the reply and speech output run locally,
exactly as in voice mode. What leaves the PC is the call's audio and the text's words, which have
to cross the phone network to reach you. That is the one exception to voice mode's "everything local" rule. It is
opt-in, and GAIA says so plainly before you turn it on (see [Privacy](#privacy)).

**GAIA only contacts you.** It calls and texts numbers you have verified, and never emergency
services or anyone else.

Related: #702 (voice-first parity), the [voice plan](/plans/voice), the
[messaging plan](/plans/messaging-integrations-plan) (alerts over Telegram and Slack).

## Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|----------|----------|
| Provider | **Telnyx**, through a SIP account the user creates. GAIA speaks plain SIP, so any SIP provider works under "Other SIP provider"; Telnyx is the one we test and document. |
| How the PC reaches the phone network | The PC **registers outbound** with the provider over SIP and places calls itself. No public URL, no webhook, no tunnel, no port forwarding. |
| Where the call runs | A small daemon sidecar, `gaia-phone`, owns SIP and audio. The call's audio feeds the same server-side voice session voice mode uses. |
| Who pays | The user's own provider account. About $1 a month for a number and about half a cent a minute in the US (Telnyx list price, November 2026). A two-minute call costs about a cent. AMD hosts nothing. |
| What counts as urgent | Only rules the user sets. GAIA never decides on its own that something deserves a call. |
| Who GAIA may call or text | Only numbers the user has verified with a test call. The sidecar enforces this, not the prompt. |
| What GAIA says before you confirm | Only that it has an urgent alert and its title. Details wait until you say "go ahead" or press 1. |
| Texts | GAIA texts the same verified numbers from the same number. Sending is an outbound HTTPS request to the provider, so it needs no public URL either. Texts are one-way at first. A messaging API is provider-specific, so texts start with Telnyx; Twilio's API is the second. |
| Text content | By default, the alert's title and "Open GAIA for details". A rule can opt in to the one-line summary. |
| Calling GAIA | Later phase: you call GAIA's number, enter your PIN, and you're in voice mode with your PC. |

### Options considered and why they lost

| Option | Why it lost |
|--------|-------------|
| **Provider media streams** (the provider calls a webhook, then opens a WebSocket to the PC for audio) | Needs a public HTTPS URL that is always up. Mobile access's tunnel is optional and starts with the Agent UI, and an alert has to work when nobody has set that up. |
| **Hosted voice-agent platforms** | They run speech recognition, speech output and often the model in the cloud. That breaks the local rule for everything said on the call, not just the audio in transit. |
| **Voice calls in messaging apps** | Telegram, Signal and Slack give bots no way to place a voice call. |
| **Push notifications only** | Silent under Do Not Disturb, which is exactly when an emergency call matters. They stay as the first, quieter step (see [Escalation](#escalation)). |
| **Texts through an email-to-SMS gateway** | Most US carriers have shut theirs down or deliver late, and none confirm delivery. |
| **Twilio as the default** | Works, but costs about three times as much per minute (about 1.4¢ in the US), and a registered SIP device on Twilio still needs a voice URL (a hosted TwiML Bin) to route its calls. It stays supported through "Other SIP provider", with setup steps in the guide. |
| **A Python SIP stack in the daemon** | The mature ones (PJSIP, pyVoIP) are GPL, which GAIA's MIT license can't take. `sipgo` (Go, BSD-2) and Pion's RTP packages (MIT) can. |

## Architecture

```
 phone ── phone network ── Telnyx ══ SIP over TLS + SRTP ══ gaia-phone (sidecar, on the PC)
                                     (PC registers outbound,           │  8 kHz audio frames
                                      so NAT is fine)                  ▼
                                                     daemon voice session (voice plan)
                                                     ASR → GAIA turn → TTS, all via Lemonade
```

- **`gaia-phone`** is a Go binary the daemon installs and supervises like its other sidecars. It
  is built on `sipgo` for SIP and Pion's `rtp` package for media, and is shipped per platform
  (Windows, Linux, macOS).
  - **Registration:** it registers with the provider on start, re-registers before expiry, and
    sends a keep-alive every 30 s so the NAT mapping stays open for incoming calls.
  - **Calls:** it places calls (INVITE with digest auth) and answers incoming ones in the later
    phase.
  - **Audio:** it carries G.711 µ-law, which every PSTN path supports, and Opus or G.722 when the
    provider offers them end to end.
  - **What it doesn't own:** it holds no model and no prompt. It turns RTP into 20 ms PCM frames
    on a loopback socket to the daemon, and back.
- **The voice session** gains a phone transport next to the browser and TUI ones. The turn loop,
  barge-in and the [spoken-reply contract](/plans/voice#the-spoken-reply-contract) are reused
  unchanged. The phone side cancels its own echo, so the session's echo handling is off for calls.
  - **Audio rates:** incoming 8 kHz audio is upsampled to 16 kHz for recognition, and speech output
    is downsampled to 8 kHz before it goes out.
- **The credentials** (SIP username and password) live in the OS credential store through the
  daemon, like provider API keys. The daemon hands them to the sidecar at spawn. They are never
  written to a file or logged.
- **The PC has to be on.** The PC places the call, so a PC that is asleep or off can't call. When
  any call rule is on, GAIA keeps the PC from sleeping while it's plugged in, and Settings says so.
  A PC that is offline can't send the alert that it's offline; see the open questions.

## When GAIA calls

### Call rules

Settings › Phone › **Call me when…** holds the rules. Each rule is a condition GAIA checks
directly in code. The model doesn't judge whether the condition is met:

| Rule | Condition |
|------|-----------|
| A scheduled job fails | A `gaia schedule` run ends in an error or its own "call me if" check matches |
| An email from someone on my list arrives | Email triage marks it urgent and the sender is on the list |
| A task waits on my approval | A background task has waited on an approval longer than N minutes (default 15) |
| GAIA itself needs attention | The daemon can't reach Lemonade, the disk is nearly full, or a sidecar keeps crashing |

**Background tasks and schedules can also call.** A skill or schedule the user runs in the
background can be given permission to call ("May call me if it needs me"). It then gets a
`call_owner(title, why)` tool. The tool goes through the same gate as every other route:
verified numbers, quiet hours and the daily cap all apply. An email or web page that tells the
agent to call can't widen any of that. The worst it can do is use one of today's calls, and every
call is logged with the rule or task that placed it.

### Escalation

Each rule picks how hard GAIA tries:

1. **Notify.** An alert in the Agent UI, the TUI, and any connected messaging app (Telegram,
   Slack).
2. **Text.** A text to your phone, right away or when nobody opens the alert within N minutes
   (default 5).
3. **Call** if nobody opens the alert within N more minutes (default 5), or **call now** for rules
   marked urgent.
4. **Call again** once if nobody answers, two minutes later. On iPhone, a second call within three
   minutes rings through Focus when "Allow repeated calls" is on, and setup turns it on.

Each rule picks the steps it uses: notify only, notify and text, or all of them. Opening the
alert in any app stops the steps that haven't run yet.

**Limits:**
- **Quiet hours:** rules marked urgent ignore them, and every other rule waits for them to end.
- **Daily cap:** 5 calls and 20 texts a day by default.
- **One call at a time:** another alert that comes in during a call is read out on that call.

## The call

1. **Ring.** Caller ID shows GAIA's number. Setup has you save it as a contact, with Emergency
   Bypass on iPhone or as a starred contact allowed through Do Not Disturb on Android.
2. **Answer.** GAIA says: "Hi, it's GAIA on your PC. I have an urgent alert: the nightly backup
   failed. Say 'go ahead' or press 1 to hear it."
   - Nothing past the title is said before you confirm, so a family member or a lock-screen
     preview hears only the title.
3. **Details.** GAIA gives the spoken summary and then stays in voice mode. You can ask
   ("what was the error?", "did last night's run work?") and interrupt at any time.
   - Answers follow the spoken-reply contract: short, with no paths or tables read out.
4. **Actions.** At first you can only ask questions on a call; actions wait until you're at the
   PC. In the later phase, which needs voice approvals (out of scope for voice mode's first
   version), a user who sets a PIN can approve a waiting task by saying it or keying it in.
   GAIA reads back what it will do before it does it. Caller ID can be spoofed, so a PIN is
   always required for actions.
5. **Hang up.** Say "that's all" or just hang up. The call is saved as a chat named
   "Call · nightly backup failed" with the transcript, the tool steps and the result, the same way
   voice turns land in history.

**Voicemail.** When the call is answered by a greeting that runs past about 4 s with no break,
GAIA treats it as voicemail. It leaves only "GAIA has an urgent alert for you. Open GAIA to see
it." and hangs up. It never says the title, because voicemail is easy for someone else to hear.

**Latency.** The phone network adds about 150 to 300 ms each way, so GAIA's answer starts about
2 s after you stop talking, against 1.5 s in voice mode at the PC. Every other part of the
voice-mode latency budget is unchanged.

## Texts

A text reads like this:

> GAIA: Nightly backup failed. Open GAIA for details.

- **What's in it:** the title is all a text says by default, because texts show on the lock
  screen and stay on the carrier's servers. A rule can opt in to the one-line summary, such as
  "GAIA: Nightly backup failed: the backup drive is full." Texts never include paths, file
  contents or links.
- **How it's sent:** the `gaia-phone` sidecar sends it through the provider's messaging API over
  HTTPS from the PC, through the same verified-number and cap checks as calls. It needs a
  provider API key next to the SIP login, kept in the OS credential store the same way.
- **Delivery:** the provider accepting a text doesn't mean it reached the phone. Delivery reports
  arrive by webhook, which needs a public URL, so at first the alert shows "Text sent" rather than
  "Delivered". That is also why texts never stop the escalation; only opening the alert does.
- **Replies:** replies also arrive by webhook, so texts are one-way at first. Once calling GAIA is on
  (the later phase), every text ends with "Call this number to talk to GAIA." Replying by text is
  an open question below.
- **US registration:** US carriers block texts from a local number that isn't registered for
  10DLC. A personal user registers as a sole proprietor: about $4 once, $15 for the campaign review,
  about $2 a month, and usually a few business days for carrier approval (Telnyx list prices,
  November 2026). A toll-free number is the other path: free to verify, about 5 business days.
  Setup walks through one of them and keeps texts off until the provider reports the number
  approved. Calls work in the meantime.

### Calling GAIA (later phase)

You call GAIA's number. The sidecar accepts calls only from verified numbers, and caller ID can be
spoofed, so it then asks for the PIN. Three wrong PINs block that number for an hour and alert you
in the apps. After the PIN you're in an ordinary voice-mode session with your PC.

## Setup

Setup is in Agent UI Settings › Phone, and the TUI has the same steps. You never use a command
line:

1. **Explain.** One screen: what calls are for, what it costs (shown from the provider's list
   price), and that call audio crosses the phone network.
2. **Account.** Create a Telnyx account (the screen links to it). Set a monthly spending limit
   and limit outbound calls to your country; setup checks both and says which one is missing.
3. **Number and SIP login.** Buy a number in the Telnyx portal, then create a SIP credential
   connection with that number on it. Paste the SIP username, password and number into GAIA.
   - GAIA registers right away and shows "Connected" or the provider's exact error. For example, a
     403 is shown as "The provider rejected the password", not as a generic failure.
4. **Your number.** Enter your phone number. GAIA calls it and reads a four-digit code; you type
   the code into Settings to verify the number. You can add more numbers.
5. **Texts (optional).** Paste a provider API key, then register the number for texts: setup links
   to the provider's sole-proprietor 10DLC form or toll-free verification, and shows "Waiting for
   approval" until the provider reports it approved.
6. **Rules.** Pick the rules, quiet hours and the daily caps.
7. **Test.** One button places a real call through the whole path: a sample alert, a follow-up
   question, and the saved chat. A second sends a test text once texts are approved.

"Other SIP provider" takes a SIP server address and credentials in place of step 3, and links to
the guide's provider notes, Twilio among them.

## Failure states

Every failure is shown in Settings › Phone, and as an alert in the apps when it hits a real
call. A failed call never quietly turns into a text or an email.

| Failure | What the user sees |
|---------|--------------------|
| Registration fails | "GAIA can't sign in to your phone provider: <provider reason>." Calls stay off until it signs in. |
| Network drops during a call | GAIA re-dials once. If that also fails, the alert stays in the apps, marked "Call failed". |
| Provider refuses the call (no balance, blocked destination) | The provider's reason, plus a link to the account page where it can be fixed. |
| Lemonade is down when a call is due | GAIA still calls and plays a short prerecorded line: "GAIA has an urgent alert but can't talk right now. Check your PC." The "GAIA itself needs attention" rule covers this case. |
| Daily cap reached | The alert stays in the apps with "Not called: daily limit reached" or "Not texted: daily limit reached". |
| Text rejected (number not registered, carrier blocked it) | The provider's reason. Texts turn off until it's fixed, and the rule's other steps still run. |

## Privacy

- **What crosses the network:** the call's audio, a text's words, and the numbers involved. The
  phone network carries audio and texts unencrypted once they leave the provider, and carriers
  keep texts. Between the PC and the provider, SIP runs over TLS, audio over SRTP, and the
  messaging API over HTTPS.
- **What never leaves the PC:** the models, the transcript, the saved chat, your files, and the
  rules.
- **How much is said:** GAIA says as little as it can. On a call you hear the title before you
  confirm, and the spoken summary after. A text carries the title, or the summary if the rule opts
  in. Files and long output stay in the saved chat.
- **What the setup notice says:** the provider can technically record calls, and GAIA never asks
  it to. It also says that phone calls are the one part of voice that isn't fully local.

## Test strategy

- **Unit tests for the sidecar** against a local SIP test server: registration, re-registration,
  digest auth, INVITE, BYE, keep-alive, DTMF, and refusing an unverified number.
- **Text tests:** the shape of the messaging API request, title-only content by default, the cap,
  and refusing an unverified number.
- **Contract tests** that check the shape of the SIP messages and SDP sent, not only that a send
  happened, because every provider rejects some malformed request a stub would accept.
- **One real call per release** to a test number on the self-hosted pool's Telnyx account:
  register, call, answer through a second SIP endpoint, play a sample alert, take a spoken
  question, hang up, check the saved chat. One real text to the same test number.
- **Gate tests:** prompt-injected email text tries to trigger a call, an unverified number, quiet
  hours, the daily cap, and a wrong PIN.
- **Hands-on pass:** iPhone and Android with Focus or Do Not Disturb on, voicemail, and a call over
  a weak connection.

## Out of scope and open questions

- **Replying by text and delivery reports.** Both arrive by webhook. Open question: receive them
  through mobile access's tunnel when it's on, or poll the provider's message records if it offers
  them, without making either a requirement.
- **Calling or texting anyone but you,** such as a family member or a colleague, or emergency
  services. Contacting other people raises consent and robocall rules that need their own design.
- **A PC that is off or offline.** An alert like "my PC went offline" has to come from somewhere
  else, such as a provider-side health check or a phone app. Open question: is it worth a small
  hosted check, and who would run it?
- **Countries.** Number and caller-ID rules vary by country. The first release targets the US and
  Canada; others follow the provider's coverage, tested one at a time.
- **Arabic.** Calls follow voice mode's Arabic support and are English only until spoken Arabic
  replies are good enough. Texts can be in Arabic from the start.
